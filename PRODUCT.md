# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is the general Swedish public, with journalists and policymakers as important secondary audiences. Visitors need a clear, credible briefing they can understand and pass on without having to adopt a party-political position.

## Product Purpose

This political-awareness campaign raises public awareness of Palantir's presence in Sweden and the Swedish Police Authority's reported use of its Acus analysis platform. It asks why this company is being used when other countries are moving away from it, and why Swedish authorities have not answered straightforward questions about the arrangement. Success means people share the campaign and its social assets, extending informed public scrutiny.

## Positioning

An evidence-led public campaign that makes the unanswered questions around Palantir's role in Swedish policing understandable, shareable, and difficult to ignore.

## Conversion & proof

- The primary action is to share the campaign link; a secondary action is to download and share campaign social assets.
- The line a visitor should remember after ten seconds: “What does Acus do, where is the data stored, and who examined the system?”
- Belief ladder: published reporting describes Acus and the supplier relationship; the Police Authority has not publicly described it; formal parliamentary questions remain unanswered; a system of this reach deserves a public explanation.
- Proof on hand: Swedish news articles, parliamentary records, government documents, and direct links to every source used.

## Brand Personality

Honest, nonpartisan, and curious. The campaign should invite scrutiny rather than prescribe ideology, with a calm seriousness that respects visitors' ability to examine evidence and reach their own conclusions.

## Anti-references

Avoid party-political propaganda, social-justice-warrior signalling, sensational tabloid treatment, and the visual language of a SaaS technology company.

## Design Principles

1. Put defensible evidence before rhetoric.
2. Make the central questions legible before introducing complexity.
3. Keep the campaign nonpartisan in tone while remaining direct about accountability.
4. Make sharing an informed action, not an act of outrage.
5. Treat uncertainty and unanswered questions as material facts, not gaps to conceal.
6. Work only from public records, published reporting, and documentation available for scrutiny; never solicit leaked information or private submissions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Maintain responsive single-column reading on mobile, 44px minimum control targets, visible keyboard focus, named evidence links, correct document language after route selection, and support for reduced motion.
