# Overall Impression

The foundation is credible, calm, and unusually disciplined about evidence. The biggest opportunity is to turn it from a source archive that happens to campaign into a campaign with a clear narrative, a concrete ending, and an archive behind it.

# What’s Working

1. **Evidence hierarchy:** publisher-named citations, explicit uncertainty, and the Known/Unknown split support the nonpartisan positioning.
2. **The Gunnar Strömmer moment:** quotation, portrait, attribution, context, and source form the strongest human and evidentiary focal point.
3. **Responsive foundation:** titles wrap, cards do not clip, one-column collapse is clean, and focus/reduced-motion foundations exist.

# Cognitive Load

Three checklist failures produce **moderate cognitive load**:

- **Single focus:** Home competes between six navigation destinations, two hero CTAs, five press exits, and later internal CTAs.
- **Minimal choices:** the desktop header/hero and the five equally weighted press links both exceed four visible options.
- **Progressive disclosure:** Home presents press, stats, quotation, five questions, convergence, disclaimer, and sharing instead of sequencing a shorter argument.

Grouping, prose measure, source recognition, and asked/answered co-location work well. Mobile is the weak state: five full image cards delay the campaign’s own evidence and create repeated parsing cost.

# Emotional Journey

Home starts with curiosity, diverts immediately into third-party reporting, regains force through the numbers, peaks at the ministerial quote and convergence argument, then ends weakly. “Pass the question on” loops visitors back into more reading instead of letting them share.

The quote/portrait is the emotional high point. The end state should convert that informed concern into a concrete action and confirmation. Known, Timeline, Acus, and Questions build trust, but their repeated heading–lead–ruled-list cadence creates evidence fatigue. About improves trust through method and privacy language, then weakens it by inviting corrections without a clear mechanism.

# Priority Issues

## [P1] The site still invites people who “hold something” to get in touch

**Why it matters:** `questions.researchBlock.lead` says, in both languages, that anyone who possesses material relevant to the headings should contact the campaign. That contradicts the explicit no-leaks/no-whistleblowing direction and creates a legal, editorial, and trust risk even though no contact form exists.

**Fix:** Remove the solicitation entirely. Describe the research agenda in terms of public records, published documentation, and the campaign’s own work.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## [P1] Visible publication-QA residue damages credibility

**Why it matters:** The signature Gunnar Strömmer quote publicly says it must be checked against the source before publication. Partial and unverified dates also appear in a campaign whose authority depends on verification.

**Fix:** Verify or remove the quote and load-bearing dates before release. Keep internal editorial status out of public copy; public uncertainty should explain what is unknowable, not expose unfinished workflow.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## [P1] The stated primary conversion does not exist

**Why it matters:** `PRODUCT.md` defines sharing as the primary action, but “Pass the question on” only links to the summary and sources. The site earns attention and then offers no copy-link, native share, social asset, or success confirmation.

**Fix:** Provide a real closing action: copy link with confirmation, native Share where supported, and a small approved asset set. Keep sources secondary.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

## [P1] The complete press archive arrives before the campaign’s argument

**Why it matters:** Five large cards are the first section after the hero and dominate the early mobile journey. Visitors are encouraged to leave before seeing the numbers, minister’s answer, unanswered questions, or synthesis. The same archive then repeats on About.

**Fix:** Keep the complete static archive on About. On Home, move press after the core evidence and use a compact proof treatment that still exposes all required articles without five consecutive large cards.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

## [P2] The identity is coherent but too close to a generic dark tech-documentation template

**Why it matters:** The current Geist/navy/cyan/mono/rule system is trustworthy but anonymous and close to the anti-reference of a technology-company microsite. Repeated Alex Karp imagery makes the press grid feel duplicated.

**Fix:** Art-direct around real public-record material: parliamentary excerpts, dates, redaction/document geometry, and restrained institutional photography. Vary page composition where the content warrants it, especially Acus and Timeline.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder`

# Minor Observations

- English press cards retain Swedish titles without identifying the destination language.
- Active desktop navigation relies mostly on a subtle color change.
- About’s desktop prose floats in a large unused field.
- Known’s side-by-side lists can imply row correspondence even though none exists.
- Acus is the most conceptually demanding and visually least articulated route; it needs one precise data-flow explanation.
- External articles, citations, and sources open new tabs without a visible warning.
- Dark mode feels heavier and more secretive than the “honest, nonpartisan, curious” personality; light mode better matches that tone.

# Questions to Consider

3. Should the next pass prioritize credibility/content hardening, narrative/conversion, or visual distinctiveness?
4. Should fixes cover all five priority issues, or the four P1 issues first?
